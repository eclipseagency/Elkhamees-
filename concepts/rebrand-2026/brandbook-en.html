The Mark

The letter khā,
with a stone for its dot.

The mark is a letter, not a picture of a product. It is the khā — the first letter of Alkhamees — drawn as a single stroke whose weight swells and thins the way a broad-nib pen lays it down. The dot above it is a four-faceted diamond: it points at the stone without drawing one. The letter's internal proportions are set on a division of five, a quiet nod to the name that lives in the construction rather than on the surface. Everything else is deliberately absent — the mark has to read at a glance, with no caption to explain it.

Lockups

Three lockups,
and no fourth.

The primary signature stacks the mark above the name — Arabic first, Latin beneath it, both centred on the mark's axis. The horizontal lockup exists for wide, shallow spaces: shopfront fascias, email footers, the top of an invoice. Below 12 mm the seal stands alone; at that size the wordmark closes up and stops being legible. Nothing else is authorised, and no one recombines these parts on the fly.

Palette

Ivory and gold outside,
burgundy inside.

Gold is one flat colour with no gradient. A gradient is an attempt to imitate the shine of metal on a flat surface, and it never convinces; a single flat tone does, because it is what actually gets foiled onto paper and engraved into the metal itself. Burgundy is the interior colour only — box lining, the back panel of a vitrine, the surface a piece is photographed on. It appears the moment a box opens, which is what makes it land: colour arriving after the ivory calm outside.

MUSAID ALKHAMEES04
Typography

A composed Arabic hand,
and a high-contrast Latin signature.

The first proposal replaced the Arabic wordmark with a plain geometric Kufi. That direction was dropped on review: stripped-back geometric Kufi reads as a property developer or a bank, not as an old family house. So the name keeps an Arabic face with real character — a modern Kufi with open counters and decided corners — and the modernity is carried by the mark and the Latin instead. Latin is a signature voice: Didot on certificates, cards and the fascia, never for running text.
